Magnesium

Keeps your muscles and nerves working, and helps control blood sugar and blood pressure.

Daily need (adults)310–420 mg a day
Upper limit350 mg (supplements)
Best foodsPumpkin seeds, chia, almonds
Pay attention ifPeople with type 2 diabetes
FoodsHow muchWho's at riskSigns of low levelsSupplementsFormsMythsFAQ

What it does

  • Helps hundreds of enzymes do their jobs
  • Keeps muscles and nerves working
  • Helps control blood sugar and blood pressure
  • Helps build bone, protein and DNA

🤓 Did you know?

Magnesium sits at the center of chlorophyll, the green pigment in plants. Leafy greens are literally built around it.

⚠️ Upper limit

350 mg a day from supplements and medicines only. Magnesium from food doesn't count toward it. More often causes diarrhea.

Who's most at risk of running low?

People with type 2 diabetesMore magnesium is lost in urine.
Older adultsThey tend to eat less, absorb less and lose more.
Gut conditionsCrohn's, celiac disease or gut surgery.
Heavy drinkingAlcohol use disorder is linked to low magnesium.
Long-term acid reducersPPIs taken for over a year can lower magnesium.

Signs you might be low

  • Loss of appetite or nausea
  • Tiredness and weakness
  • Muscle cramps, numbness or tingling
  • Irregular heartbeat (in severe cases)

Healthy people rarely get symptoms, because the kidneys hold on to magnesium when intake is low.

🩺 When to see a doctor

These signs have many possible causes. If they last more than a couple of weeks, see a doctor. A simple blood test can often check your levels. Don't guess with high-dose supplements.

Supplements: do you need one?

When it helps

Most people can get enough from nuts, seeds, beans and whole grains. Your doctor may suggest one if you take a PPI long term or have a gut condition. Magnesium for migraine prevention uses doses above the upper limit, so only with a doctor's guidance.

Choosing one

Citrate, aspartate, lactate and chloride forms are absorbed better than oxide. Labels list elemental magnesium. Diarrhea is the most common side effect.

💊 Medicine interactions

  • Bisphosphonates for osteoporosis (like alendronate): take them at least 2 hours apart
  • Antibiotics like doxycycline or ciprofloxacin: take the antibiotic 2 hours before or 4–6 hours after magnesium
  • Water pills: loop and thiazide diuretics raise magnesium losses; potassium-sparing ones (spironolactone) lower them
  • Acid reducers (PPIs) taken for over a year can lower magnesium
  • Kidney disease: avoid magnesium supplements unless prescribed

Always tell your doctor and pharmacist about any supplements you take.

Forms of Magnesium

Food is the easy route. Supplement forms differ in how well they're absorbed.

  • Magnesium in foodIn food

    Seeds, nuts, beans, whole grains and greens.

  • Citrate, aspartate, lactate, chlorideSupplements

    Absorbed better than oxide or sulfate in small studies.

  • Oxide and sulfateSupplements

    Absorbed less, and more likely to cause diarrhea. Also used as laxatives and antacids.

Myths vs facts

MythMagnesium is a proven sleep aid.
FactThere isn't strong trial evidence. Getting enough from food is great; a supplement for sleep is a maybe at best.
MythAntacids and laxatives are a harmless way to get magnesium.
FactSome, like milk of magnesia, can far exceed the upper limit, and they're risky if your kidneys don't work well.

Frequently asked questions

Does magnesium lower blood pressure?

Only a little, about 2–4 points in studies. The FDA calls the evidence inconsistent. Magnesium-rich foods are still worth eating.

Can magnesium help migraines?

Small trials suggest it may reduce how often they happen, and neurology groups call it probably effective. The doses used are above the upper limit, so only with your doctor's supervision.
