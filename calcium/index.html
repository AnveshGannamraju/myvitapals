Calcium

Builds strong bones and teeth, and helps your muscles, heart and nerves work.

Daily need (adults)1,000–1,200 mg a day
Upper limit2,000–2,500 mg
Best foodsYogurt, milk, fortified soy milk
Pay attention ifWomen after menopause
FoodsHow muchWho's at riskSigns of low levelsSupplementsFormsMythsFAQ

What it does

  • Builds and maintains bones and teeth
  • Helps muscles contract, including your heart
  • Helps nerves send signals
  • Helps blood clot

🤓 Did you know?

Your blood calcium stays almost perfectly steady. If you don't eat enough, your body quietly borrows it from your bones.

👩‍🍳 Kitchen tips

  • Spread calcium through the day. Your body absorbs it best in amounts of 500 mg or less at a time.
  • Vitamin D helps you absorb calcium.
  • A bowl of curd plus a glass of milk covers more than half a day's need.

How much do you need?

Adults 19–501,000 mg
Men 51–701,000 mg
Women 51+1,200 mg
Everyone 71+1,200 mg
Pregnancy & breastfeeding1,000 mg

Recommended daily amounts for adults, from the US Food and Nutrition Board.

⚠️ Upper limit

2,500 mg a day up to age 50, then 2,000 mg. This includes food and supplements.

Who's most at risk of running low?

Women after menopauseLess estrogen means less calcium absorbed and more bone loss.
People who avoid dairyIncluding lactose intolerance, milk allergy and vegan diets.
Adults over 50Needs rise and absorption drops with age.

Signs you might be low

  • Usually no symptoms for years
  • Bone loss (osteoporosis) and fractures
  • Tingling in the hands, feet or around the mouth (if blood calcium drops)
  • Muscle cramps or spasms

Low intake mostly shows up silently as weaker bones over years. Blood calcium stays normal even when you eat too little, so ask about a bone density (DEXA) scan if you're at risk.

🩺 When to see a doctor

These signs have many possible causes. If they last more than a couple of weeks, see a doctor. A simple blood test can often check your levels. Don't guess with high-dose supplements.

Supplements: do you need one?

When it helps

If food doesn't get you close, for example if you skip dairy and fortified foods. Many people only need a small top-up, not a full 1,000 mg pill.

Choosing one

Calcium carbonate is about 40% calcium and works best with food. Calcium citrate is about 21% calcium, works without food and is gentler on the stomach. Take 500 mg or less at a time.

💊 Medicine interactions

  • Levothyroxine (thyroid medicine): take it at least 4 hours apart from calcium
  • Antibiotics like ciprofloxacin: take them 2 hours before or 2 hours after calcium
  • Dolutegravir (for HIV): take it 2 hours before or 6 hours after calcium
  • Lithium: may raise blood calcium, and extra calcium could add to it

Always tell your doctor and pharmacist about any supplements you take.

Forms of Calcium

Calcium in food is easy. Supplements mostly come in two forms.

  • Calcium in foodIn food

    Dairy, fortified foods, calcium-set tofu, sardines and some greens.

  • Calcium carbonateSupplements

    About 40% calcium. Cheaper, but take it with food. Can cause gas or constipation.

  • Calcium citrateSupplements

    About 21% calcium. Works without food and is gentler on the stomach.

Myths vs facts

MythMore calcium pills mean stronger bones.
FactAbove your daily need, extra calcium doesn't build more bone, and high supplement doses may raise kidney stone risk for some people.
MythOnly dairy has calcium.
FactFortified soy milk, calcium-set tofu, ragi, til, sardines with bones and greens like sarson all help.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a calcium supplement after menopause?

Not automatically. Add up what you eat first: three servings of dairy or fortified alternatives cover most of the 1,200 mg. Ask your doctor about bone density testing.

Is calcium bad for my heart?

Studies are mixed. An expert panel concluded that intakes within the upper limit are safe for the heart. Food sources are the safest bet.
