## 02-vitamin-d-sunny
Sunshine doesn't guarantee vitamin D. ☀️
Indoor jobs, clothing, darker skin and pollution all cut how much your skin makes.
Adults need 600–800 IU a day, and the upper limit is 4,000 IU.
In a risk group? Ask your doctor about a blood test.
Full guide: link in bio.

## 03-b12-vegetarians
Vegetarian? This one's for you. 🥛
Plants don't make B12. Dairy helps, but many vegetarians still run low.
Adults need 2.4 mcg a day. A simple blood test tells you where you stand.
Full B12 guide: link in bio.

## 04-iron-spinach
Sorry, Popeye. 🥬
Spinach has some iron, but your body absorbs plant iron less. Dals, beans and fortified cereals do as much or more.
Pro tip: lemon on your dal, chai between meals.
Tired all the time? Get a blood test before taking iron.
Link in bio.

## 05-vitamin-c-colds
Does vitamin C stop colds? For most people, no. 🍊
Regular intake may make colds slightly shorter. That's it.
75–90 mg a day is easy from food: one orange, some amla or guava.
Over 2,000 mg a day can upset your stomach.
Link in bio.

## 06-calcium-curd
A bowl of curd and a glass of milk covers more than half your daily calcium. 🥣
No dairy? Fortified soy milk, calcium-set tofu, ragi and til all help.
Your body absorbs it best in small amounts, so spread it through the day.
Link in bio.

## 07-upper-limits
More isn't always better. ⚠️
Most vitamins and minerals have a daily upper limit, and it's easy to cross by stacking a multivitamin, single pills and fortified foods.
Save this for the next time you're in the supplement aisle.
Link in bio.

## 08-medicine-interactions
Your medicine and your vitamins may not get along. 💊
Metformin, acid reducers, thyroid pills, steroids and some antibiotics all interact with common nutrients.
Never change a medicine on your own. Tell your doctor and pharmacist about every supplement you take.
Link in bio.

## 09-magnesium-rice
Brown rice has about 4× the magnesium of white rice. 🍚
Refining strips most of it out. Whole wheat atta, bajra, jowar, nuts and seeds bring it back.
Adults need 310–420 mg a day.
