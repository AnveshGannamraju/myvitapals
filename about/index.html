    About MyVitaPals

    MyVitaPals explains vitamins in plain language, so you can make sense of what you eat and have better conversations with your doctor.

    Who it's for

    MyVitaPals is written for adults aged 20 and over. Children and teens have different needs and safe limits, so please ask a pediatrician about them.

    Food first, science always

    Most people can get most vitamins from a varied diet. We show real foods, with real amounts, before we ever talk about supplements. We include everyday US and Indian foods, because that's how many of our readers eat.

    Where our facts come from

    Daily needs, upper limits, food amounts and medicine interactions come from the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements fact sheets for health professionals, which are based on the US Food and Nutrition Board. Every page links to its source.

    How we rate evidence

    Topic badges are editorial summaries, not a validated evidence-grading system or proof of causation. Read the underlying sources and context. Clinical review and a claim-by-claim evidence appraisal remain pending:

    • 🟢 Established guidance: summarizes guidance discussed in the page sources; this does not establish that every associated benefit is causal.
    • 🟡 Limited evidence: promising or consistent studies, but smaller, mixed or mostly observational.
    • ⚪ Not enough evidence: too little good research to say either way.
    • 🔴 Myth: the evidence says no, or it isn't a recognized condition.

    How we keep it current

    Dates indicate content updates, not clinical review. No qualified clinical reviewer is currently credited. Medical and dietetic review remains pending, especially for pregnancy, supplement doses and medication interactions.

    What we don't do

    We don't diagnose, and we don't recommend specific supplement brands. Our vitamin check is educational. It can point you toward questions to ask, but assessment may include diet, symptoms, medical history and appropriate laboratory testing.

    Corrections and contact

    Report a content error or contact the project maintainer on GitHub (account required). Include the page URL, the wording and a reliable source. Reports are public: do not include personal health information. Reports can be tracked in the repository issue list.

    Privacy and disclosures

    Searches and tool answers are processed in your browser. Saving questionnaire answers is optional and uses this browser’s local storage; use Clear saved answers to remove them. The site uses no advertising trackers and no cookies. To learn which pages help people, it counts visits with GoatCounter, a privacy-friendly analytics service: the page address (without anything after a "?"), the referring website, screen size, which links are clicked, the matched food from our list when the food finder is used, and whether the vitamin check was started and completed. It never records what you type, your vitamin check answers or results, your weight, or anything that identifies you, and it is switched off if your browser sends Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control. The hosting provider may process ordinary request logs. External links follow the destination’s privacy policy. No supplement affiliate purchase links are used in these tools.

    Editorial accountability

    Content is maintained by the MyVitaPals project. No clinical credentials or independent medical review are claimed. A content update is an edit; a fact-check verifies a specific claim against its source; medical review requires a qualified named professional and a documented scope.

    Medical disclaimer

    MyVitaPals is for general education and isn't a substitute for professional medical advice. Talk to a doctor, registered dietitian or pharmacist before starting a supplement, especially if you're pregnant, breastfeeding, take medicines or have a health condition.
