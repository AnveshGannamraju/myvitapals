    Dals & beans

    The backbone of vegetarian protein: cheap, filling and packed with fiber, iron and folate.

    What makes it special

    • Good protein plus fiber, iron, folate and potassium
    • Low in one amino acid (methionine), which grains supply, so dal-chawal and rajma-roti are a great team
    • Very filling for the calories
    • Cooked dal from about 50 g dry gives roughly 11 g of protein

    🤓 Did you know?

    ICMR-NIN's 2020 numbers assume a cereal, legume and milk mix of roughly 3:1:2.5. In other words, more dal and milk relative to rice and roti than many plates have.

    👍 Why it's worth eating

    Fiber, iron and folate come along for free. A thicker dal (more dal, less water) is the easiest protein upgrade in an Indian kitchen.

    ⚠️ What to watch

    Beans can cause gas at first. Increase slowly, soak well and cook thoroughly. Pair with vitamin C (lemon, tomato, amla) to absorb more of their iron.

    💡 Easy win

    Keep roasted chana or sprouts on hand. They're a protein snack that needs no cooking.

    📏 How much protein do you need?

    Compare the traditional adult RDA (0.8 g/kg/day) with the 2025–2030 US dietary target (1.2–1.6 g/kg/day). Age, activity and medical circumstances affect individual advice.
