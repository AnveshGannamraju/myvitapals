Protein source · Whey, casein, soy, pea

Protein powders

Convenient protein when food falls short. Helpful for some, unnecessary for most.

What makes it special

  • Whey and casein come from milk; soy and pea are plant-based
  • Whey is digested fast; casein is digested slowly
  • A scoop usually gives 20–30 g, but check the label
  • Food first: Indian guidelines from ICMR-NIN advise against them for healthy people

🤓 Did you know?

Whey used to be a waste product of cheese and paneer making. Now it's one of the best-selling supplements in the world.

👍 Why it's worth eating

Useful if your appetite is poor, if you're older and struggle to eat enough, during hard training, or when food isn't practical. The Australian Institute of Sport suggests 20–40 g per serve and no more than 1–2 serves a day.

⚠️ What to watch

In a 2025 Consumer Reports test of 23 powders and shakes, more than two-thirds had more lead per serving than its experts consider safe for a day. Plant-based powders averaged about 9 times more lead than dairy-based ones. Many also add sugar, caffeine or herbal extracts.

🏷️ Label decoder

Look for a third-party seal: NSF Certified for Sport, Informed Choice or USP. These test for contaminants and banned substances. Avoid products with a California Prop 65 lead warning for daily use.

📏 How much protein do you need?

About 0.8 g per kg of body weight a day for most adults: 46–56 g a day. More if you're over 65 or train hard.
