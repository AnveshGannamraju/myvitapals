Thinking about supplements

Food first. Most people get enough of most vitamins from a varied diet. But some people really do benefit from a supplement, and some supplements can cause harm.

The vitamins to focus on

Vitamin B12Vegans, many vegetarians, adults over 50, and people on long-term metformin or acid reducers.
Vitamin B9Anyone who could become pregnant: 400 mcg of folic acid daily.
Vitamin DPeople with little sun, darker skin, or older age.

How to choose a good supplement

Look for a sealUSP Verified, NSF Certified or ConsumerLab means the label matches what's inside
Skip megadosesMore than 100% of the daily value is rarely needed unless your doctor says so
Add it all upCount vitamins across every product you take, including 'energy', 'hair' or 'immunity' blends
Tell your doctorAnd your pharmacist, especially if you take any medicines

📌 Good to know

Red flags: any product that claims to 'cure', 'detox' or 'boost immunity overnight', or that warns you not to tell your doctor. Real vitamins don't need hype.

🩺 Talk to your doctor about

  • Ask whether a blood test would help before starting B12 or vitamin D.
  • Ask your pharmacist to check for interactions with your medicines.
  • Bring your supplement bottles to checkups.

About & how we write

VitaPals is written for adults aged 20 and over, for general education, not medical advice. Facts are based on the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements. Talk to a doctor, dietitian or pharmacist before starting a supplement, especially if you're pregnant, take medicines or have a health condition.
