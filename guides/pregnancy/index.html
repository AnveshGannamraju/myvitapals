Pregnant or planning

Some vitamins matter even before you know you're pregnant. Here's what the evidence says. Your doctor or midwife will tailor it to you.

The vitamins to focus on

Vitamin B9Take 400 mcg of folic acid every day, starting at least a month before pregnancy. It helps prevent serious birth defects of the brain and spine. Needs rise to 600 mcg in pregnancy.
Vitamin B12Needs rise to 2.6 mcg. Vegetarians and vegans should make sure they're covered, since low B12 affects baby's development.
Vitamin D600 IU a day. Many people start pregnancy low, so ask about testing.
Vitamin AYou need some, but avoid high-dose retinol supplements and large amounts of liver, which can cause birth defects. Beta-carotene from vegetables is safe.
Vitamin CNeeds rise a little, to 85 mg. Easy with fruit and veg.

Smart moves

Before pregnancyStart a folic acid supplement (or a prenatal vitamin that has it)
Every dayLeafy greens, dals and beans for natural folate
Check labelsMake sure you're not doubling up vitamin A from several supplements
Morning sicknessAsk your doctor before trying B6 or ginger

📌 Good to know

Prenatal vitamins also contain iron, iodine and sometimes choline and DHA. All of these matter in pregnancy.

🩺 Talk to your doctor about

  • Choose a prenatal vitamin together with your doctor.
  • Mention any other supplements so you don't exceed safe limits.
  • If you're vegetarian or vegan, ask about B12 testing.

Sources: NIH Office of Dietary Supplements fact sheets: Vitamin B9, Vitamin B12, Vitamin A, Vitamin D. Last reviewed October 2026. General education, not medical advice.
