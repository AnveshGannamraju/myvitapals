Vitamin A

Helps you see in the dark, keeps your skin and eyes healthy, and helps your body fight germs.

Daily need (adults)700–900 mcg a day
Upper limit3,000 mcg (retinol only)
Best foodsSweet potato, spinach, carrots
Pay attention ifPeople with gut conditions that limit fat absorption
FoodsHow muchWho's at riskSigns of low levelsSupplementsFormsMythsFAQ
RetinolVitamin A that's ready to use the moment you eat it. It comes from animal foods.
CarotenoidsColorful plant pigments that your body turns into vitamin A only when it needs it.

What it does

  • Helps your eyes see in dim light
  • Keeps your skin and the lining of your nose and gut healthy
  • Helps your immune system fight germs
  • Helps your body make and repair cells

🤓 Did you know?

The story goes that in World War II, Britain told everyone its pilots ate lots of carrots to see at night. The real secret was radar! Carrots do help your eyes, but they won't give you night vision.

👩‍🍳 Kitchen tips

  • Beta-carotene needs a little fat to be absorbed. A drizzle of oil or ghee on carrots and greens helps.
  • Cooking carrots and spinach lightly actually makes their beta-carotene easier to absorb.

How much do you need?

Men900 mcg
Women700 mcg
Pregnancy770 mcg
Breastfeeding1,300 mcg

Recommended daily amounts for adults, from the US Food and Nutrition Board.

⚠️ Upper limit

3,000 mcg a day of preformed vitamin A (retinol) from supplements and animal foods. There's no upper limit for beta-carotene from food.

Who's most at risk of running low?

People with gut conditions that limit fat absorptionSuch as cystic fibrosis, celiac disease or Crohn's disease.
Pregnant and breastfeeding women in low-income regionsWhere diets have little variety.

Signs you might be low

  • Trouble seeing when it gets dark
  • Dry eyes
  • Getting sick more often

Vitamin A deficiency is rare in the US but still common in some low- and middle-income countries.

🩺 When to see a doctor

These signs have many possible causes. If they last more than a couple of weeks, see a doctor. A simple blood test can often check your levels. Don't guess with high-dose supplements.

Supplements: do you need one?

When it helps

Most people get enough from food. Supplements are rarely needed unless a doctor finds a deficiency.

Choosing one

If your multivitamin has vitamin A, check how much is retinol (retinyl acetate or palmitate) versus beta-carotene. Retinol is the part that counts toward the upper limit.

💊 Medicine interactions

  • Orlistat (Alli, Xenical), a weight-loss drug, lowers vitamin A absorption
  • Retinoid medicines like acitretin (Soriatane) and bexarotene (Targretin) shouldn't be combined with vitamin A supplements

Always tell your doctor and pharmacist about any supplements you take.

Forms of Vitamin A

Vitamin A comes in two big groups: ready-made vitamin A from animal foods, and plant pigments your body turns into vitamin A.

  • Retinol & retinyl estersIn food

    Ready-made ('preformed') vitamin A. Found in eggs, milk, cheese and fish.

  • Beta-caroteneIn food

    The orange plant pigment your body turns into vitamin A. Carrots, sweet potatoes and mangoes are loaded with it.

  • Alpha-carotene & beta-cryptoxanthinIn food

    Beta-carotene's cousins. Your body turns them into vitamin A too. Find them in pumpkin, papaya and tangerines.

  • Retinal & retinoic acidActive form

    The active forms your body makes from retinol. Retinal is the one that helps your eyes see in dim light.

  • Lutein, zeaxanthin & lycopeneNot vitamin A

    Lookalikes! These carotenoids never become vitamin A, but they're still good for you.

Myths vs facts

MythCarrots give you night vision.
FactVitamin A keeps normal night vision working. It won't give you super-vision. That story was WWII propaganda to hide radar.
MythMore vitamin A means better skin and eyes.
FactToo much retinol from supplements can harm your liver and bones, and cause birth defects in pregnancy.

Frequently asked questions

Is beta-carotene safe?

Beta-carotene from food is safe. Your body only converts what it needs. High-dose beta-carotene supplements raised lung cancer risk in smokers in large studies, so smokers should avoid them.

Should I avoid vitamin A in pregnancy?

You need some vitamin A in pregnancy, but avoid high-dose retinol supplements and liver in large amounts. Ask your doctor whether your prenatal vitamin uses beta-carotene.
