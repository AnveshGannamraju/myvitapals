    Vitamin B6

    Helps your body use protein and cooks up brain chemicals that affect mood and sleep.

    Vitamin B6 in 30 seconds

    Vitamin B6 helps more than 100 enzymes do their jobs, mostly in breaking down and using protein. It also helps make brain messengers such as serotonin, and supports immune function and red blood cells. Adults need 1.3 mg a day (1.5–1.7 mg after 50). Chickpeas, fish, potatoes and bananas are top sources. Long-term high doses from supplements can damage nerves.

    What does vitamin B6 do?

    • Helps make serotonin and dopamine, your mood messengers
    • Helps make melatonin (hello, sleep!)
    • Supports your immune system and red blood cells

    🤓 Did you know?

    B6 isn't just one thing. It's a family of six closely related compounds.

    🌱 Vegetarian & vegan sources

    Chickpeas are one of the best sources (chole, chana chaat or hummus), along with potatoes, bananas, peanuts and fortified cereal. Vegetarians and vegans can meet B6 needs from food.

    How much vitamin B6 do you need a day?

    Adults 19–501.3 mg
    Men 51+1.7 mg
    Women 51+1.5 mg
    Pregnancy1.9 mg
    Breastfeeding2.0 mg

    Recommended daily amounts for adults, from the US Food and Nutrition Board.

    ⚠️ Upper limit

    The U.S. adult upper limit is 100 mg/day; EFSA uses 12 mg/day for adults, including pregnancy and breastfeeding. These are ceilings, not targets. Supplemental B6 can cause peripheral neuropathy; check combined products and seek care for tingling, burning or numbness.

    Who might run low on vitamin B6?

    Kidney diseaseIncluding people on dialysis.
    Autoimmune conditionsSuch as rheumatoid arthritis or celiac disease.
    Heavy alcohol useAlcohol breaks down B6 faster.

    Signs of low vitamin B6

    • Itchy, scaly rash
    • Cracked lips and sore tongue
    • Feeling low or confused

    Low B6 often happens alongside low B12 or folate.

    🩺 When to see a doctor

    These signs have many possible causes. If they last more than a couple of weeks, see a doctor. Assessment depends on diet, symptoms and history; laboratory tests are useful for some nutrients. Don't guess with high-dose supplements.

    Do you need a vitamin B6 supplement?

    When it helps

    Not needed for most people. Check how much B6 is in any 'energy', 'stress' or multivitamin product. It adds up across products.

    Choosing one

    Pyridoxine (pyridoxine HCl) is the most common form. More is NOT better.

    💊 Medicine interactions

    • Some seizure medicines (valproate, carbamazepine, phenytoin) can lower B6
    • Cycloserine (a tuberculosis drug) and theophylline (an asthma drug) can lower B6

    Always tell your doctor and pharmacist about any supplements you take.

    Forms of Vitamin B6

    'Vitamin B6' is a team name for six closely related compounds: three main forms, each with a phosphate version.

    • PyridoxineIn food

      Found in plant foods. Also the most common form in supplements, as pyridoxine hydrochloride (HCl).

    • PyridoxalIn food

      Found mostly in animal foods.

    • PyridoxamineIn food

      Found mostly in animal foods.

    • PLP & PMPActive form

      The active forms your body makes: pyridoxal 5'-phosphate and pyridoxamine 5'-phosphate. PLP is the busy one.

    Vitamin B6 myths vs facts

    MythWater-soluble vitamins can't hurt you.
    FactLong-term high-dose B6 can cause numbness and nerve damage.

    Vitamin B6: frequently asked questions

    What is the function of vitamin B6?

    It acts as a helper (coenzyme) for more than 100 enzyme reactions, mostly involved in protein metabolism. It's also needed for brain development and immune function.

    Does B6 help with morning sickness?

    Doctors sometimes recommend B6 for nausea in pregnancy, but only take it on their advice.

    How do I know if I'm taking too much B6?

    Add up the B6 in every supplement you take. If you have tingling or numbness in your hands or feet, stop and see a doctor.
