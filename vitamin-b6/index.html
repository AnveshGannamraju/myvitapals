Vitamin B6

Helps your body use protein and cooks up brain chemicals that affect mood and sleep.

🧑‍🍳 Nickname: The Mood Chef
Daily need (adults)1.3–1.7 mg a day
Upper limit100 mg
Best foodsChickpeas, fish, potatoes
Pay attention ifKidney disease
FoodsHow muchWho's at riskSigns of low levelsSupplementsFormsMythsFAQ

What it does

  • Helps make serotonin and dopamine, your mood messengers
  • Helps make melatonin (hello, sleep!)
  • Supports your immune system and red blood cells

🤓 Did you know?

B6 isn't just one thing. It's a family of six closely related compounds. Overachiever!

How much do you need?

Adults 19–501.3 mg
Men 51+1.7 mg
Women 51+1.5 mg
Pregnancy1.9 mg
Breastfeeding2.0 mg

Recommended daily amounts for adults, from the US Food and Nutrition Board.

⚠️ Upper limit

100 mg a day. Nerve damage has been reported with long-term high doses, and some health agencies now warn about lower doses too.

Who's most at risk of running low?

Kidney diseaseIncluding people on dialysis.
Autoimmune conditionsSuch as rheumatoid arthritis or celiac disease.
Heavy alcohol useAlcohol breaks down B6 faster.

Signs you might be low

  • Itchy, scaly rash
  • Cracked lips and sore tongue
  • Feeling low or confused

Low B6 often happens alongside low B12 or folate.

🩺 When to see a doctor

These signs have many possible causes. If they last more than a couple of weeks, see a doctor. A simple blood test can often check your levels. Don't guess with high-dose supplements.

Supplements: do you need one?

When it helps

Not needed for most people. Check how much B6 is in any 'energy', 'stress' or multivitamin product. It adds up across products.

Choosing one

Pyridoxine (pyridoxine HCl) is the most common form. More is NOT better.

💊 Medicine interactions

  • Some seizure medicines (valproate, carbamazepine, phenytoin) can lower B6
  • Cycloserine (a tuberculosis drug) and theophylline (an asthma drug) can lower B6

Always tell your doctor and pharmacist about any supplements you take.

Forms of Vitamin B6

'Vitamin B6' is a team name for six closely related compounds: three main forms, each with a phosphate version.

  • PyridoxineIn food

    Found in plant foods. Also the most common form in supplements, as pyridoxine hydrochloride (HCl).

  • PyridoxalIn food

    Found mostly in animal foods.

  • PyridoxamineIn food

    Found mostly in animal foods.

  • PLP & PMPActive form

    The active forms your body makes: pyridoxal 5'-phosphate and pyridoxamine 5'-phosphate. PLP is the busy one.

Myths vs facts

MythWater-soluble vitamins can't hurt you.
FactLong-term high-dose B6 can cause numbness and nerve damage.

Frequently asked questions

Does B6 help with morning sickness?

Doctors sometimes recommend B6 for nausea in pregnancy, but only take it on their advice.

How do I know if I'm taking too much B6?

Add up the B6 in every supplement you take. If you have tingling or numbness in your hands or feet, stop and see a doctor.
