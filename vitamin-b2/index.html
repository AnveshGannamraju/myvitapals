Riboflavin · Water-soluble

Vitamin B2

Helps every cell make energy and keeps your skin, eyes and mouth in good shape.

Daily need (adults)1.1–1.3 mg a day
Upper limitNone set
Best foodsDairy, fortified oats, almonds
Pay attention ifVegans and people who drink little milk
FoodsHow muchWho's at riskSigns of low levelsSupplementsFormsMythsFAQ

What it does

  • Powers energy-making inside your cells
  • Helps keep skin and eyes healthy
  • Helps other B vitamins do their jobs (team player!)

🤓 Did you know?

Ever taken a multivitamin and then seen neon-yellow pee? That's extra B2 leaving your body. Harmless.

👩‍🍳 Kitchen tips

  • Light breaks down riboflavin, so store milk in opaque containers.
  • Dairy is the main source for many people. If you skip dairy, look for fortified plant milks.

How much do you need?

Men1.3 mg
Women1.1 mg
Pregnancy1.4 mg
Breastfeeding1.6 mg

Recommended daily amounts for adults, from the US Food and Nutrition Board.

ℹ️ Upper limit

No upper limit has been set. Extra is flushed out in urine.

Who's most at risk of running low?

Vegans and people who drink little milkDairy is a major source.
Vegetarian athletesThey may need a little more.
Pregnancy and breastfeedingNeeds go up.

Signs you might be low

  • Cracks at the corners of your mouth
  • Sore, red tongue or throat
  • Itchy eyes that dislike bright light

Deficiency is rare in countries where milk and fortified foods are common.

🩺 When to see a doctor

These signs have many possible causes. If they last more than a couple of weeks, see a doctor. A simple blood test can often check your levels. Don't guess with high-dose supplements.

Supplements: do you need one?

When it helps

Rarely needed. High-dose riboflavin is sometimes used for migraine prevention, but only with a doctor's guidance.

Choosing one

Riboflavin is the usual form. Bright yellow urine afterward is normal.

💊 Medicine interactions

  • No clinically important medicine interactions are known

Always tell your doctor and pharmacist about any supplements you take.

Forms of Vitamin B2

Riboflavin is the food form. Your body turns it into two helper molecules that do the real work.

  • RiboflavinIn food

    The form in food and supplements, and the reason multivitamins turn pee neon yellow.

  • FMN (flavin mononucleotide)Active form

    An active helper made from riboflavin. It helps your cells make energy.

  • FAD (flavin adenine dinucleotide)Active form

    The other active helper. It powers energy-making and helps other B vitamins work.

Myths vs facts

MythNeon-yellow pee means the vitamins aren't working.
FactIt's just extra riboflavin leaving your body. Totally normal.

Frequently asked questions

Can riboflavin help migraines?

Some studies suggest high-dose riboflavin may reduce migraines in some adults. Talk to your doctor before trying it.
