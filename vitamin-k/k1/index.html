Vitamin K · K1 · Phylloquinone

The Leafy One

The main kind of vitamin K in food, made by green plants.

🧈 Fat-soluble

🔍 What makes it special

  • Made by green plants as part of photosynthesis
  • Your liver uses it to make the proteins that help blood clot
  • Your body turns some of it into K2 (MK-4)

🍽️ Find me in

🥬Kale & spinach
🌿Collard & turnip greens
🥦Broccoli
🥗Lettuce
🫒Soybean & canola oil

🏷️ Label decoder

Labels say 'phylloquinone' or 'phytonadione'. Phytonadione is the medicine name, and it's the kind in the vitamin K shot newborns get.

🤓 Fun fact

Vitamin K is fat-soluble, so a drizzle of oil on your greens helps you absorb more of it. Salad dressing, you're actually helping!

⚠️ Heads-up

On a blood thinner like warfarin? Keep the amount of leafy greens you eat steady from day to day, and talk to your doctor before making big changes.

📏 How much do I need?

Adult men need about 120 mcg a day and women about 90 mcg. Half a cup of cooked spinach or collard greens can cover several days' worth.
