Vitamin K

Helps your blood clot so cuts stop bleeding, and helps keep your bones strong.

🩹 Nickname: The Patch-Up Pro
Daily need (adults)90–120 mcg a day
Upper limitNone set
Best foodsLeafy greens, broccoli, soybeans
Pay attention ifNewborns who didn't get the vitamin K shot
FoodsHow muchWho's at riskSigns of low levelsSupplementsFormsMythsFAQ
K1The main kind of vitamin K in food, made by green plants.
K2A family of vitamin K forms made by bacteria. Find them in fermented foods and some animal foods.

What it does

  • Helps blood clot so cuts stop bleeding
  • Helps build strong bones
  • Teams up with calcium and vitamin D

🤓 Did you know?

The 'K' comes from the German word 'Koagulation' (clotting). Babies are born with very little, so most newborns get a vitamin K shot right after birth.

👩‍🍳 Kitchen tips

  • Vitamin K needs fat to be absorbed. Cooking greens with a little oil helps.
  • On warfarin? The key is consistency: similar amounts of greens each week.

How much do you need?

Men120 mcg
Women90 mcg
Pregnancy & breastfeeding90 mcg

Recommended daily amounts for adults, from the US Food and Nutrition Board.

ℹ️ Upper limit

No upper limit has been set. Vitamin K from food and supplements has low potential for harm.

Who's most at risk of running low?

Newborns who didn't get the vitamin K shotBabies are born with very little.
Gut conditions that limit absorptionSuch as celiac disease or cystic fibrosis.

Signs you might be low

  • Bruising easily
  • Bleeding that's slow to stop, like nosebleeds or bleeding gums

Deficiency in healthy adults is rare.

🩺 When to see a doctor

These signs have many possible causes. If they last more than a couple of weeks, see a doctor. A simple blood test can often check your levels. Don't guess with high-dose supplements.

Supplements: do you need one?

When it helps

Not needed for most adults. Don't start or stop vitamin K if you take warfarin without talking to your doctor.

Choosing one

K1 (phylloquinone) is the main food form. K2 (MK-7) is common in supplements.

💊 Medicine interactions

  • ⚠️ Warfarin (Coumadin) and similar blood thinners: sudden changes in vitamin K change how the drug works
  • Some antibiotics may lower vitamin K made by gut bacteria
  • Cholesterol binders (cholestyramine, colestipol) and orlistat lower absorption

Always tell your doctor and pharmacist about any supplements you take.

Forms of Vitamin K

Vitamin K has two natural forms: K1 from plants, and K2 from bacteria and animal foods.

  • Vitamin K1 (phylloquinone)In food

    The main form in food, mostly from green leafy veggies.

  • Vitamin K2 (menaquinones)In food

    A whole series, MK-4 through MK-13. Found in fermented foods, cheese and eggs, and made by the bacteria in your gut.

  • MK-4Made by your body

    A K2 form your body makes from K1.

  • MK-7In food

    A K2 form packed into natto, a sticky fermented soybean dish from Japan.

  • Vitamin K3 (menadione)Retired

    A lab-made form. It's no longer used in supplements because it harmed liver cells in lab studies.

Myths vs facts

MythPeople on blood thinners must avoid green vegetables.
FactNo. Keep intake steady from week to week, and your doctor adjusts your dose.

Frequently asked questions

Does K2 help bones?

Research is promising but not conclusive. Food first. Ask your doctor before supplementing.

Is natto worth trying?

It's the richest K2 food by far. It's an acquired taste, though!
