Vitamin K · Phylloquinone & menaquinone

The Patch-Up Pro

Helps your blood clot so cuts stop bleeding, and helps keep your bones strong.

🧈 Fat-soluble

💪 Superpowers

  • Helps blood clot so cuts stop bleeding
  • Helps build strong bones
  • Teams up with calcium and vitamin D

🍽️ Find me in

🥬Kale & spinach
🥦Broccoli
🥗Lettuce
🫘Soybeans
🌿Collard & turnip greens
🫒Soybean & canola oil

🪫 Running low? You might notice

  • Bruising easily
  • Bleeding that's slow to stop, like nosebleeds or bleeding gums

📏 How much do I need?

Adult men need about 120 mcg a day and women about 90 mcg. Half a cup of cooked spinach or collard greens can cover several days' worth.

🤓 Fun fact

The 'K' comes from the German word 'Koagulation' (clotting). Babies are born with very little, so most newborns get a vitamin K shot right after birth.

⚠️ Heads-up

Taking a blood thinner like warfarin? Don't stop eating greens. Just keep the amount you eat steady from day to day, and talk to your doctor before making big changes.

🧩 Meet the family: forms of vitamin K

Vitamin K has two natural forms: K1 from plants, and K2 from bacteria and animal foods.

  • Vitamin K1 (phylloquinone)In food

    The main form in food, mostly from green leafy veggies.

  • Vitamin K2 (menaquinones)In food

    A whole series, MK-4 through MK-13. Found in fermented foods, cheese and eggs, and made by the bacteria in your gut.

  • MK-4Made by your body

    A K2 form your body makes from K1.

  • MK-7In food

    A K2 form packed into natto, a sticky fermented soybean dish from Japan.

  • Vitamin K3 (menadione)Retired

    A lab-made form. It's no longer used in supplements because it harmed liver cells in lab studies.

📚 Sources: NIH Office of Dietary Supplements — Vitamin K fact sheet and health professional fact sheet. Amounts are general daily needs for healthy adults. Last reviewed October 2026.
