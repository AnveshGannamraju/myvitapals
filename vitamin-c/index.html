Ascorbic acid · Water-soluble

Vitamin C

Builds collagen (your body's glue), helps heal cuts and keeps your immune system ready for action.

🦸 Nickname: The Citrus Superhero
Daily need (adults)75–90 mg a day
Upper limit2,000 mg
Best foodsBell peppers, oranges, kiwi
Pay attention ifSmokers and people around secondhand smoke
FoodsHow muchWho's at riskSigns of low levelsSupplementsFormsMythsFAQ

What it does

  • Builds collagen, the glue for your skin, gums, blood vessels and bones
  • Helps cuts and scrapes heal
  • Supports your immune system
  • Helps your body soak up iron from plant foods

🤓 Did you know?

Most animals make their own vitamin C, but humans can't. Neither can guinea pigs or fruit bats. Old-time sailors got sick on long trips until they packed limes, which is how British sailors got the nickname 'limeys'.

👩‍🍳 Kitchen tips

  • Heat and water destroy vitamin C. Eat some fruit and veg raw.
  • Squeeze lemon on dal or sabzi at the end of cooking, not during.
  • Vitamin C helps you absorb iron from plant foods. Pair them!

How much do you need?

Men90 mg
Women75 mg
Pregnancy85 mg
Breastfeeding120 mg
SmokersAdd 35 mg

Recommended daily amounts for adults, from the US Food and Nutrition Board.

⚠️ Upper limit

2,000 mg a day. More can cause diarrhea and stomach upset.

Who's most at risk of running low?

Smokers and people around secondhand smokeSmoking uses up vitamin C faster.
Very limited dietsIncluding some older adults and people with food insecurity.
Some gut and kidney conditionsIncluding people on dialysis.

Signs you might be low

  • Bleeding or swollen gums
  • Bruising easily
  • Cuts that heal slowly
  • Feeling tired

Severe deficiency (scurvy) is rare but still happens with very restricted diets.

🩺 When to see a doctor

These signs have many possible causes. If they last more than a couple of weeks, see a doctor. A simple blood test can often check your levels. Don't guess with high-dose supplements.

Supplements: do you need one?

When it helps

Most people can easily get enough from fruits and vegetables. A supplement may help smokers or people who eat very little produce.

Choosing one

Ascorbic acid works fine. Buffered forms (sodium or calcium ascorbate) may be gentler on the stomach.

💊 Medicine interactions

  • Some chemotherapy and radiation treatments: ask your oncologist before taking antioxidant supplements
  • Combination with niacin and simvastatin may blunt cholesterol benefits

Always tell your doctor and pharmacist about any supplements you take.

Forms of Vitamin C

Vitamin C is mostly one molecule, but it comes in a few supplement styles.

  • Ascorbic acidIn food

    The natural form in fruits and veggies, and the most common supplement form.

  • Sodium & calcium ascorbateSupplements

    Ascorbic acid paired with a mineral. They're gentler on the stomach and absorbed about the same.

  • Dehydroascorbic acidMade by your body

    The 'used-up' form after vitamin C fights damage. Your body can recycle it back.

Myths vs facts

MythVitamin C prevents colds.
FactFor most people it doesn't prevent colds, but regular intake may make them slightly shorter.
MythMegadoses are harmless because extra is peed out.
FactOver 2,000 mg a day can cause diarrhea and raise kidney stone risk in some people.

Frequently asked questions

Is amla really high in vitamin C?

Yes, amla is one of the richest fruit sources. Fresh is best, since processing and heat reduce it.

Does orange juice count?

Yes. ¾ cup gives about a day's worth, but whole fruit also gives fiber.
