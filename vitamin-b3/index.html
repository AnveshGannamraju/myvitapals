Niacin · Water-soluble

Vitamin B3

Helps turn food into energy and keeps your skin, gut and brain running smoothly.

🔥 Nickname: The Fire Starter
Daily need (adults)14–16 mg a day
Upper limit35 mg (supplements)
Best foodsChicken, fish, peanuts
Pay attention ifPeople who are undernourished
FoodsHow muchWho's at riskSigns of low levelsSupplementsFormsMythsFAQ

What it does

  • Helps hundreds of enzymes do their jobs
  • Supports healthy skin
  • Keeps digestion and nerves working well

🤓 Did you know?

Your body can build its own niacin from tryptophan, the protein piece famous (unfairly!) for making people sleepy after big holiday dinners.

How much do you need?

Men16 mg
Women14 mg
Pregnancy18 mg
Breastfeeding17 mg

Recommended daily amounts for adults, from the US Food and Nutrition Board.

⚠️ Upper limit

35 mg a day from supplements and fortified foods. This doesn't apply to niacin prescribed by a doctor.

Who's most at risk of running low?

People who are undernourishedLow overall food intake.
Low intake of B2, B6 or ironYour body needs them to make niacin from tryptophan.
Rare conditionsSuch as Hartnup disease or carcinoid syndrome.

Signs you might be low

  • Rough skin rash that gets worse in the sun
  • Upset stomach
  • Feeling foggy or confused

Severe deficiency (pellagra) causes a sun-sensitive rash, diarrhea and confusion. It's rare today.

🩺 When to see a doctor

These signs have many possible causes. If they last more than a couple of weeks, see a doctor. A simple blood test can often check your levels. Don't guess with high-dose supplements.

Supplements: do you need one?

When it helps

Not needed for most people. High-dose niacin is a prescription cholesterol treatment and should only be taken under a doctor's care.

Choosing one

Nicotinic acid can cause flushing. Nicotinamide (niacinamide) doesn't. 'Flush-free' inositol hexanicotinate is absorbed less well.

💊 Medicine interactions

  • Isoniazid and pyrazinamide (tuberculosis drugs) can lower niacin
  • High-dose nicotinic acid can raise blood sugar, affecting diabetes medicines

Always tell your doctor and pharmacist about any supplements you take.

Forms of Vitamin B3

Niacin has two main forms. They do the same job, but only one makes you blush!

  • Nicotinic acidIn food

    Found in food and supplements. Big doses cause the famous red, tingly 'niacin flush'.

  • Nicotinamide (niacinamide)In food

    Does the same job without the flush. You'll also spot it in lots of skin creams.

  • NAD & NADPActive form

    The active forms your body builds from niacin. They help hundreds of enzymes turn food into energy.

  • TryptophanMade by your body

    Not niacin itself, but your body can turn this protein piece into niacin.

Myths vs facts

MythNiacin flush means it's detoxing you.
FactFlushing is just widened blood vessels. It isn't a detox.

Frequently asked questions

Is niacinamide in skin care the same as niacin?

It's a form of vitamin B3, but skin creams act on the skin. They don't count toward your daily needs.

Should I take niacin for cholesterol?

Only if your doctor prescribes it. High doses can cause liver problems and raise blood sugar.
