ALA, EPA & DHA · Essential fats

Omega-3

Healthy fats that build your cell membranes, especially in the brain and eyes, and support your heart.

Daily need (adults)1.1–1.6 g ALA a day
Upper limitNone set
Best foodsFlaxseed, chia, salmon
Pay attention ifVery low-fat diets
FoodsHow muchWho's at riskSigns of low levelsSupplementsFormsMythsFAQ

What it does

  • Build cell membranes, especially in the brain and eyes
  • EPA and DHA lower triglycerides (a blood fat)
  • Support a baby's brain and eye development
  • Help make signals for your heart, blood vessels and immune system

🤓 Did you know?

DHA is concentrated in your retina and brain, and breast milk naturally contains it.

👩‍🍳 Kitchen tips

  • If you eat fish, aim for 1–2 servings a week, especially oily fish like salmon, mackerel and sardines.
  • Vegetarians can meet the ALA need with 1 tbsp of flaxseed or a small handful of walnuts.

How much do you need?

Men (ALA)1.6 g
Women (ALA)1.1 g
Pregnancy (ALA)1.4 g
Breastfeeding (ALA)1.3 g
EPA + DHANo official amount. The AHA suggests 1–2 servings of fish a week

Recommended daily amounts for adults, from the US Food and Nutrition Board.

ℹ️ Upper limit

No upper limit is set. The FDA considers up to 5 g a day of EPA + DHA from supplements safe.

Who's most at risk of running low?

Very low-fat dietsFat is where omega-3s come from.
Poor fat absorptionSome gut conditions limit how much fat you absorb.
People who don't eat fishALA is easy to get, but EPA and DHA come mainly from seafood.

Signs you might be low

  • Rough, scaly skin
  • A red, itchy rash

True deficiency is very rare in healthy people.

🩺 When to see a doctor

These signs have many possible causes. If they last more than a couple of weeks, see a doctor. A simple blood test can often check your levels. Don't guess with high-dose supplements.

Supplements: do you need one?

When it helps

Not needed by most healthy people. For people with heart disease, the AHA suggests about 1 g a day of EPA + DHA, preferably from fish. Ask your doctor before starting.

Choosing one

Fish oil, krill oil and algae oil (a vegan source) are common. Check the label for the actual EPA + DHA per serving, not just total oil.

💊 Medicine interactions

  • Warfarin and other blood thinners: high doses may increase bleeding risk; your doctor may check your INR
  • High doses (4 g a day for years) slightly raised the risk of an irregular heartbeat (atrial fibrillation) in heart patients

Always tell your doctor and pharmacist about any supplements you take.

Forms of Omega-3

There are three main omega-3s. Only ALA is strictly essential, because your body makes just a little EPA and DHA from it.

  • ALA (alpha-linolenic acid)In food

    From plants: flaxseed, chia, walnuts, canola and soybean oil.

  • EPA and DHAIn food

    From fish and seafood. These are the ones linked to heart benefits.

  • Fish, krill and algae oilSupplements

    Supplement sources. Algae oil is a vegan source of EPA and DHA.

Myths vs facts

MythFish oil pills protect everyone's heart.
FactIn the large VITAL trial, 1 g a day didn't lower major heart events overall. The AHA doesn't recommend supplements for people at low risk.
MythOmega-3 capsules fix dry eyes.
FactA large study found 3 g a day of EPA + DHA for a year worked no better than a placebo.

Frequently asked questions

Do vegetarians need fish oil?

Not automatically. The ALA need is easy to meet with flaxseed, chia or walnuts. Algae oil is a vegan EPA and DHA option if your doctor suggests one.

Is mercury in fish a worry?

Choose lower-mercury fish like salmon, sardines and canned light tuna. In pregnancy, 8–12 oz a week of low-mercury seafood is recommended.
