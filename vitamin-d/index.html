Vitamin D

Helps your body grab calcium from food to build strong bones and teeth.

Vitamin D in 30 seconds

Vitamin D helps your body absorb calcium, so it's essential for strong bones; your muscles, nerves and immune system need it too. Adults need 600 IU (15 mcg) a day, or 800 IU after 70. Few foods have much: fatty fish, egg yolks, fortified milk and UV-exposed mushrooms lead the list. Most people also rely on some sun or a supplement.

What does vitamin D do?

  • Helps your body absorb calcium for strong bones and teeth
  • Keeps your muscles working
  • Supports your immune system

🤓 Did you know?

Your skin can make vitamin D from sunlight. That's why it's called the 'sunshine vitamin'. It also works more like a hormone than a regular vitamin.

👩‍🍳 Kitchen tips

  • Skin produces vitamin D with UV exposure, but production varies widely. Prioritize food and fortification, and protect your skin; do not seek extra unprotected sun to meet vitamin D needs.
  • Vitamin D is fat-soluble, so take supplements with a meal.

🌱 Vegetarian & vegan sources

Eggless vegetarians and vegans have fewer food options: fortified milk or plant milk (about 100–144 IU a cup; check the label), UV-exposed mushrooms (vitamin D2) and fortified cereal. Eggs add about 44 IU each. In India, look for the +F logo on milk and cooking oil: FSSAI standards allow them to be fortified with vitamins A and D. Vegan supplements use D2 or lichen-based D3.

How much vitamin D do you need a day?

Adults 20–70600 IU (15 mcg)
Over 70800 IU (20 mcg)
Pregnancy & breastfeeding600 IU (15 mcg)

Recommended daily amounts for adults, from the US Food and Nutrition Board.

⚠️ Upper limit

4,000 IU (100 mcg) a day for adults. Doctors sometimes prescribe more for a short time to treat a deficiency.

Who might run low on vitamin D?

Older adultsSkin makes less and kidneys convert less.
People who get little sunIndoor jobs, covered clothing or northern winters.
Darker skin tonesMore melanin means less vitamin D made from the same sun.
Obesity or gastric bypassVitamin D gets trapped in fat tissue or absorbed less.

Signs of low vitamin D

  • Weak or achy bones
  • Muscle weakness
  • Bone pain and soft bones (called osteomalacia in adults)

Routine vitamin D blood testing is generally not recommended for otherwise healthy adults. A clinician may test when symptoms, bone disease, malabsorption or other clinical indications make the result useful.

🩺 When to see a doctor

These signs have many possible causes. If they last more than a couple of weeks, see a doctor. Assessment depends on diet, symptoms and history; laboratory tests are useful for some nutrients. Don't guess with high-dose supplements.

Do you need a vitamin D supplement?

When it helps

Use foods and fortified foods to help meet recommended intakes. Discuss supplementation when intake is inadequate or your circumstances warrant it. Testing is not routinely required for healthy adults.

Choosing one

D3 raises blood levels more than D2. Take it with food that has some fat.

💊 Medicine interactions

  • Steroids like prednisone lower vitamin D
  • Orlistat (Alli, Xenical) lowers absorption
  • Statins may interact
  • Thiazide water pills plus vitamin D can raise calcium too high

Always tell your doctor and pharmacist about any supplements you take.

Forms of Vitamin D

There are two main kinds of vitamin D, and your body converts both into an active hormone in two steps.

  • Vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol)In food

    The kind your skin makes from sunshine. Also in fish and egg yolks, and in most supplements.

  • Vitamin D2 (ergocalciferol)In food

    Made by mushrooms and yeast in UV light. Used in some supplements and fortified foods.

  • Calcidiol (25-hydroxyvitamin D)Made by your body

    Step 1: your liver makes this form. It's what doctors measure in a vitamin D blood test.

  • CalcitriolActive form

    Step 2: your kidneys make this active hormone form. It's the one that helps you absorb calcium.

Vitamin D myths vs facts

MythLiving somewhere sunny means you have enough.
FactMany people in sunny places are low, due to indoor life, clothing, skin tone and pollution.
MythMore vitamin D is always better.
FactToo much raises blood calcium and can harm kidneys and heart.

Vitamin D: frequently asked questions

Is vitamin D3 the same as vitamin D?

D3 (cholecalciferol) is one of the two main forms of vitamin D; the other is D2 (ergocalciferol). Both count toward your daily need. See our vitamin D3 page for D3 food sources and how it compares.

D3 or D2?

Both work, but D3 raises and keeps blood levels higher. D2 is the vegan option, though vegan D3 from lichen also exists.

How much sun do I need?

It depends on skin tone, season, location and time of day. There's no single safe number, so many experts suggest food and supplements over extra sun.

Can I get enough vitamin D from Indian food?

It's hard from food alone, especially for vegetarians. Fortified milk and oil (look for +F), eggs, fish like sardines and mackerel (bangda), and UV-exposed mushrooms help. Many people also depend on sun exposure or a supplement; ask your doctor what fits you.
