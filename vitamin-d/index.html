Vitamin D

Helps your body grab calcium from food to build strong bones and teeth.

☀️ Nickname: The Sunshine Buddy
Daily need (adults)600–800 IU a day
Upper limit4,000 IU
Best foodsFatty fish, UV mushrooms, fortified milk
Pay attention ifOlder adults
FoodsHow muchWho's at riskSigns of low levelsSupplementsFormsMythsFAQ
D3The kind your skin makes from sunshine. It's also the most common kind in supplements.
D2The plant-and-fungus kind of vitamin D, made by mushrooms and yeast in UV light.

What it does

  • Helps your body absorb calcium for strong bones and teeth
  • Keeps your muscles working
  • Supports your immune system

🤓 Did you know?

Your skin can make vitamin D from sunlight. That's why it's called the 'sunshine vitamin'. And it actually works more like a hormone than a regular vitamin. Fancy!

👩‍🍳 Kitchen tips

  • Your skin makes vitamin D from sunlight, but glass and sunscreen block it. Short, sensible sun exposure helps. Avoid sunburn.
  • Vitamin D is fat-soluble, so take supplements with a meal.

How much do you need?

Adults 20–70600 IU (15 mcg)
Over 70800 IU (20 mcg)
Pregnancy & breastfeeding600 IU (15 mcg)

Recommended daily amounts for adults, from the US Food and Nutrition Board.

⚠️ Upper limit

4,000 IU (100 mcg) a day for adults. Doctors sometimes prescribe more for a short time to treat a deficiency.

Who's most at risk of running low?

Older adultsSkin makes less and kidneys convert less.
People who get little sunIndoor jobs, covered clothing or northern winters.
Darker skin tonesMore melanin means less vitamin D made from the same sun.
Obesity or gastric bypassVitamin D gets trapped in fat tissue or absorbed less.

Signs you might be low

  • Weak or achy bones
  • Muscle weakness
  • Bone pain and soft bones (called osteomalacia in adults)

Low vitamin D is common in many countries, even sunny ones like India. A blood test (25-hydroxyvitamin D) is the only way to know.

🩺 When to see a doctor

These signs have many possible causes. If they last more than a couple of weeks, see a doctor. A simple blood test can often check your levels. Don't guess with high-dose supplements.

Supplements: do you need one?

When it helps

Worth discussing with your doctor if you're in any risk group. A blood test helps decide the right dose.

Choosing one

D3 raises blood levels more than D2. Take it with food that has some fat.

💊 Medicine interactions

  • Steroids like prednisone lower vitamin D
  • Orlistat (Alli, Xenical) lowers absorption
  • Statins may interact
  • Thiazide water pills plus vitamin D can raise calcium too high

Always tell your doctor and pharmacist about any supplements you take.

Forms of Vitamin D

There are two main kinds of vitamin D, and your body converts both into an active hormone in two steps.

  • Vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol)In food

    The kind your skin makes from sunshine. Also in fish and egg yolks, and in most supplements.

  • Vitamin D2 (ergocalciferol)In food

    Made by mushrooms and yeast in UV light. Used in some supplements and fortified foods.

  • Calcidiol (25-hydroxyvitamin D)Made by your body

    Step 1: your liver makes this form. It's what doctors measure in a vitamin D blood test.

  • CalcitriolActive form

    Step 2: your kidneys make this active hormone form. It's the one that helps you absorb calcium.

Myths vs facts

MythLiving somewhere sunny means you have enough.
FactMany people in sunny places are low, due to indoor life, clothing, skin tone and pollution.
MythMore vitamin D is always better.
FactToo much raises blood calcium and can harm kidneys and heart.

Frequently asked questions

D3 or D2?

Both work, but D3 raises and keeps blood levels higher. D2 is the vegan option, though vegan D3 from lichen also exists.

How much sun do I need?

It depends on skin tone, season, location and time of day. There's no single safe number, so many experts suggest food and supplements over extra sun.
