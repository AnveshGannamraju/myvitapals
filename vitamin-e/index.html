Vitamin E

Protects your cells from damage, like a bodyguard for every cell in your body.

💪 Nickname: The Bodyguard
Daily need (adults)15 mg a day
Upper limit1,000 mg (supplements)
Best foodsSunflower seeds, almonds, plant oils
Pay attention ifGut conditions that limit fat absorption
FoodsHow muchWho's at riskSigns of low levelsSupplementsFormsMythsFAQ
TocopherolsFour forms of vitamin E, and alpha-tocopherol is the one your body keeps.
TocotrienolsFour lesser-known forms of vitamin E, found mainly in certain oils and grains.

What it does

  • Protects your cells from damage (it's an antioxidant)
  • Supports your immune system
  • Helps keep blood vessels open and blood flowing smoothly

🤓 Did you know?

Its science name, tocopherol, comes from Greek words meaning 'to bring forth babies'. It was first discovered as something rats needed to have babies!

👩‍🍳 Kitchen tips

  • A small handful of almonds or sunflower seeds a day covers about half your needs.
  • Most Americans eat less than the recommended amount, though true deficiency is rare.

How much do you need?

Adults15 mg
Pregnancy15 mg
Breastfeeding19 mg

Recommended daily amounts for adults, from the US Food and Nutrition Board.

⚠️ Upper limit

1,000 mg a day from supplements (about 1,500 IU natural or 1,100 IU synthetic).

Who's most at risk of running low?

Gut conditions that limit fat absorptionSuch as Crohn's disease or cystic fibrosis.
Very low-fat dietsVitamin E comes mostly from fats and oils.
Premature babiesBorn with low stores.

Signs you might be low

  • Very rare in healthy people
  • Muscle weakness
  • Tingling or loss of feeling
  • Trouble with eyesight

True deficiency is rare in healthy adults.

🩺 When to see a doctor

These signs have many possible causes. If they last more than a couple of weeks, see a doctor. A simple blood test can often check your levels. Don't guess with high-dose supplements.

Supplements: do you need one?

When it helps

Not recommended routinely. Large trials didn't show benefits for heart disease or cancer prevention, and in one big trial, men taking 400 IU daily had a slightly higher prostate cancer risk.

Choosing one

'd-alpha-tocopherol' is natural. 'dl-alpha-tocopherol' is synthetic and about half as strong per mg.

💊 Medicine interactions

  • Blood thinners (warfarin) and antiplatelet drugs: vitamin E can increase bleeding risk
  • Simvastatin with niacin: may blunt benefits
  • Chemotherapy and radiation: ask your oncologist first

Always tell your doctor and pharmacist about any supplements you take.

Forms of Vitamin E

Vitamin E is a family of eight! Only one form, alpha-tocopherol, counts toward your daily needs.

  • Alpha-tocopherolActive form

    The star. It's the only form your body uses to meet its vitamin E needs.

  • Beta, gamma & delta-tocopherolIn food

    Alpha's siblings. Gamma-tocopherol is the most common kind in the American diet, thanks to soybean and corn oil.

  • Tocotrienols (alpha, beta, gamma, delta)In food

    Four more cousins, found in oils like palm and rice bran oil.

  • Natural vs. lab-made labelsSupplements

    'd-alpha-tocopherol' is natural. 'dl-alpha-tocopherol' is lab-made and only about half as strong per milligram.

Myths vs facts

MythVitamin E pills prevent heart disease.
FactLarge studies found no benefit.

Frequently asked questions

Does vitamin E oil help scars?

Studies are mixed, and some people get skin irritation. It doesn't affect your body's vitamin E levels.
