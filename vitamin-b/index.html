The B vitamins

Eight vitamins that work as a team to turn food into energy, keep nerves healthy and build blood cells. Your body doesn't store most of them, so you need a steady supply from food.

All 8 at a glance

VitaminDaily needBest foods
B1 · ThiamineTurns the food you eat into fuel your body can actually use, especially carbs.1.1–1.2 mgFortified cereal, beans, whole grains
B2 · RiboflavinHelps every cell make energy and keeps your skin, eyes and mouth in good shape.1.1–1.3 mgDairy, fortified oats, almonds
B3 · NiacinHelps turn food into energy and keeps your skin, gut and brain running smoothly.14–16 mgChicken, fish, peanuts
B5 · Pantothenic acidHelps your body break down fats, carbs and protein, and helps make some hormones.5 mgMushrooms, sunflower seeds, chicken
B6 · PyridoxineHelps your body use protein and cooks up brain chemicals that affect mood and sleep.1.3–1.7 mgChickpeas, fish, potatoes
B7 · BiotinHelps turn food into energy, and is famous (maybe too famous) for hair, skin and nails.30 mcgEggs, salmon, sunflower seeds
B9 · FolateHelps make and repair DNA and build brand-new cells, including red blood cells.400 mcgSpinach, beans, asparagus
B12 · CobalaminKeeps your nerves healthy and helps make red blood cells and DNA.2.4 mcgFish, dairy, fortified foods

👀 The two to watch

B12 (vegetarians, vegans, adults over 50, metformin or acid-reducer users) and folate (anyone who could become pregnant). The others are usually easy to get from food.

🤔 Where are B4, B8, B10 and B11?

They were once called B vitamins, but later research showed they weren't true vitamins, or they were the same as others. So the numbering has gaps.

B vitamin all-stars

Foods that bring several B vitamins at once.

🫘 Beans, dals & chickpeas🌾 Whole grains & fortified cereal🥛 Milk & curd🥚 Eggs🐟 Fish🍄 Mushrooms🌻 Sunflower seeds🥬 Leafy greens

About & how we write

VitaPals is written for adults aged 20 and over, for general education, not medical advice. Facts are based on the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements. Talk to a doctor, dietitian or pharmacist before starting a supplement, especially if you're pregnant, take medicines or have a health condition.
