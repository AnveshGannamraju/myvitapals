Vitamin B12

Keeps your nerves healthy and helps make red blood cells and DNA.

🛡️ Nickname: The Nerve Guardian
Daily need (adults)2.4 mcg a day
Upper limitNone set
Best foodsFish, dairy, fortified foods
Pay attention ifVegetarians and especially vegans
FoodsHow muchWho's at riskSigns of low levelsSupplementsFormsMythsFAQ

What it does

  • Protects nerves and supports memory
  • Helps make red blood cells
  • Teams up with folate (B9) to build DNA

🤓 Did you know?

B12 is the only vitamin with a metal inside it: cobalt! And your liver can store several years' worth.

👩‍🍳 Kitchen tips

  • Plants don't make B12. Spirulina, seaweed and fermented foods are NOT reliable sources.
  • Vegetarians: 2–3 servings of dairy a day helps, but many still need fortified foods or a supplement.

How much do you need?

Adults2.4 mcg
Pregnancy2.6 mcg
Breastfeeding2.8 mcg
Adults over 50Get most from fortified foods or a supplement

Recommended daily amounts for adults, from the US Food and Nutrition Board.

ℹ️ Upper limit

No upper limit has been set. Your body absorbs only a small portion of large doses.

Who's most at risk of running low?

Vegetarians and especially vegansB12 is naturally found only in animal foods.
Adults over 50Less stomach acid means less B12 absorbed from food.
People taking metformin or acid reducers long-termBoth lower B12 absorption.
Pernicious anemia or gut surgeryThe body can't absorb B12 normally.
Vegan mothers who breastfeedLow B12 in breast milk can harm a baby's development, so mothers need enough.

Signs you might be low

  • Feeling very tired
  • Pins and needles in hands and feet
  • Memory or mood changes
  • Pale skin

Low B12 can cause permanent nerve damage if untreated, sometimes before anemia shows up. A simple blood test can check.

🩺 When to see a doctor

These signs have many possible causes. If they last more than a couple of weeks, see a doctor. A simple blood test can often check your levels. Don't guess with high-dose supplements.

Supplements: do you need one?

When it helps

Strongly recommended for vegans. Worth discussing for vegetarians, adults over 50, and anyone on long-term metformin or acid reducers.

Choosing one

Cyanocobalamin is cheap, stable and well studied. Methylcobalamin also works. Shots are used when absorption is the problem.

💊 Medicine interactions

  • Metformin (diabetes) lowers B12 over time
  • Acid reducers: PPIs like omeprazole and H2 blockers like famotidine
  • Chloramphenicol (an antibiotic)

Always tell your doctor and pharmacist about any supplements you take.

Forms of Vitamin B12

B12 has four forms. Two are active in your body, and two are mostly found in supplements and shots.

  • CyanocobalaminSupplements

    Lab-made and super stable, so it's the most common form in supplements and fortified foods. Your body converts it.

  • HydroxocobalaminSupplements

    Often used in B12 shots.

  • MethylcobalaminActive form

    An active form. It's the main one in your blood.

  • AdenosylcobalaminActive form

    The other active form. It works inside your cells' power plants (mitochondria).

Myths vs facts

MythVegetarians get enough B12 from dairy.
FactSome do, but many don't. Getting tested is the only way to know.
MythB12 shots give everyone more energy.
FactThey only help if you're low.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if I'm low on B12?

Ask your doctor for a blood test. Your doctor may also check methylmalonic acid (MMA) for a clearer picture.

Is too much B12 harmful?

No upper limit is set, and extra is mostly not absorbed. Unusually high blood levels without supplements are worth mentioning to your doctor.
