Minerals & omega-3

Your body needs minerals in small amounts to build bones, carry oxygen and run your thyroid. These six, plus omega-3 fats, are the ones people ask about most.

All 7 at a glance

NutrientAdults needBest foods
IronHelps your red blood cells carry oxygen from your lungs to every part of your body.8–18 mgFortified cereal, beans, lentilsIndia: Rajma (kidney beans)
CalciumBuilds strong bones and teeth, and helps your muscles, heart and nerves work.1,000–1,200 mgYogurt, milk, fortified soy milkIndia: Curd (dahi) & buttermilk
MagnesiumKeeps your muscles and nerves working, and helps control blood sugar and blood pressure.310–420 mgPumpkin seeds, chia, almondsIndia: Kaddu ke beej (pumpkin seeds)
ZincSupports your immune system, helps wounds heal, and keeps your sense of taste and smell working.8–11 mgOysters, beef, fortified cerealIndia: Kaddu ke beej & til (pumpkin & sesame seeds)
IodineLets your thyroid make the hormones that set how your body uses energy.150 mcgIodized salt, dairy, fishIndia: Iodized salt (namak) in home cooking
SeleniumHelps protect your cells from damage and helps your thyroid hormones work.55 mcgBrazil nuts, tuna, sardinesIndia: Eggs
Omega-3Healthy fats that build your cell membranes, especially in the brain and eyes, and support your heart.1.1–1.6 g ALAFlaxseed, chia, salmonIndia: Alsi (flaxseed), roasted or ground

Daily needs for adults from the US Food and Nutrition Board, via the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements.

👀 The three to watch

Iron (heavy periods, pregnancy, vegetarians), calcium (no dairy, women after menopause) and iodine (no iodized salt, pregnancy). The others are usually easy to get from a varied diet.

⚠️ More isn't better

Minerals have upper limits, and some build up. Iron, selenium and zinc pills can cause real harm in high doses. Test before you take, and keep iron away from children.
